Key Finding

Gross Medicare Part D spending on GLP-1 drugs grew 5× from 2019 to 2023, driven almost entirely by three newer agents: Ozempic (+1,567%), Rybelsus (+2,182%), and Mounjaro (launched 2022, +1,541% by 2023). Legacy GLP-1s like Trulicity and Victoza are in structural decline. Medicare currently covers these drugs only for Type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, obesity coverage remains prohibited by law, meaning the $9.2B Ozempic spend in 2023 reflects just one of its three indications. The BALANCE model launching 2027 and negotiated Ozempic pricing (~$274/30-day supply effective 2027) will materially reshape this spending trajectory.

Why Spending is Rising
Three compounding drivers
1. Utilization — more beneficiaries
Ozempic enrollees: 142K (2019) → 1.5M (2023) → 2M (2024). Medicare Part D enrollment grew only 12% in the same period, GLP-1 growth is driven by adoption, not population growth.
2. Price — high list prices, rising annually
Ozempic list price: $676 (2017) → $969 (2024). Mounjaro: $974 (2022) → $1,069 (2023). These are list prices; net prices after rebates are lower but rebate data is not publicly available.
3. Indication expansion — more covered uses
Ozempic approved for CVD in 2024, expanding the covered patient pool. Obesity coverage still prohibited but BALANCE model (2027) will add a major new indication.

So What — Implications for Health Plan Analytics

GLP-1 spending is the defining drug trend in Medicare Part D for 2020–2030. Three signals health analytics teams should monitor: (1) Indication creep, as CVD and sleep apnea coverage expands, the covered population grows without any change in obesity rules; watch claims with newly-eligible ICD-10 codes (I10–I15, G47). (2) Rebate opacity, all publicly-reported figures are gross; net spending after manufacturer rebates is estimated at 40–50% lower, which changes the cost-effectiveness calculation for plan formulary decisions. (3) BALANCE model 2027, voluntary but likely to accelerate utilization substantially; plans should model their GLP-1 cost exposure scenarios now using current enrollment + estimated obesity prevalence. For a health plan like TRS serving ~250K active and retired members, a 5% uptake rate at $274/month = ~$41M additional annual drug spend.
