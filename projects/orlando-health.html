The Problem

Where Revenue Was Leaking Out

When I started pulling the Epic EHR billing data, the picture that came back was a scheduling process quietly leaking revenue. Imaging orders scheduled outside their insurance authorization windows were getting denied, and nobody had connected the denials back to the scheduling behavior causing them. The manual DAR scheduling system left too much room for inaccuracy, and no one was measuring what it was costing in unpaid claims.

The goal was to get specific. Not just "claims are being denied" but which ones, why, and what fixing the root cause would actually be worth in paid claims and revenue.

Before — Manual DAR Scheduling
  • 73.6% insurance payment rate across 36,589 imaging orders
  • No enforcement of insurance authorization windows at scheduling
  • Claim denials untracked and unlinked to scheduling behavior
  • Average paid claim of $238 with revenue lost to preventable denials
  • No visibility into payment performance by location or priority
After — Work Queue Enforcement (Mar 1 Go-Live)
  • Payment rate improved to 74.9% (+1.3pp) across 57,908 orders
  • 251 additional paid claims per month, $64K monthly revenue recovered
  • Average reimbursement per case up $16.96
  • Lead time and STAT scheduling held steady, no operational trade-off
  • Dual-audience Power BI dashboard tracking payment KPIs by location

Methodology

How I Worked Through It

I followed the DMAIC structure from the beginning. Before touching any data, I spent time with the clinical and administrative staff who actually lived inside the process every day. Those conversations shaped everything that came after. They flagged things the data alone would never have surfaced, and they helped me understand which metrics actually mattered to the people doing the work versus the ones that looked good in a report.

From there I pulled 94,500 billing records from Epic EHR, engineered scheduling accuracy flags in SQL, and built the before/after KPI framework that became the backbone of the analysis. The measurements tracked insurance payment rate, scheduling accuracy, lead time, and reimbursement per case, all split around the March 1 Work Queue go-live to isolate the system's true impact.

Key Learnings

What I Took Away From This

The biggest thing this internship taught me is that the hardest part of healthcare analytics is not the querying. It is knowing which questions to ask before you write a single line of SQL. The Define and Measure phases felt slow at the time, but every conversation with clinical staff narrowed my focus in ways the data alone could not. I would have built the wrong dashboard if I had started with the data instead of the people.

The other thing that stuck with me was what it felt like when the analysis actually landed. The gap analysis was not just a report that got filed somewhere. It was the specific evidence that supported expanding the Work Queue rollout across imaging service lines. That is a different kind of outcome than producing a clean chart, and it is the kind of work I want to keep doing.
